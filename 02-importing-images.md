# 🖼️ How to Import Images into HueForge

## Introduction

One of the most important steps in creating beautiful multi-color 3D prints is choosing the right image.

In this tutorial, you'll learn how to import images into HueForge, prepare them for printing, and improve image quality before adjusting filament colors.

## Step 1: Choose the Right Image

Not every image will produce the same results in HueForge.

For beginners, I recommend starting with images that have:

- **High resolution:** Clear images help preserve recognizable details.
- **Good contrast:** Distinct light and dark areas make it easier to create depth and definition.
- **Simple backgrounds:** Busy backgrounds can make important details harder to distinguish.
- **Recognizable subjects:** Portraits, animals, landscapes, and illustrations are great starting points.

### Beginner Tip

If you're new to HueForge, try using an illustration with a limited color palette before attempting a detailed photograph.

## Step 2: Prepare Your Image

Before importing your image, consider making a few adjustments.

1. Crop unnecessary background elements.
2. Adjust brightness if the image is too dark.
3. Increase contrast if important details blend together.
4. Make sure the subject is centered.
5. Save your edited image as a PNG or JPG.

**Why this matters:** HueForge relies on image colors and brightness to help generate layered artwork. A well-prepared image makes it easier to achieve recognizable results.

## Step 3: Import Your Image into HueForge

1. Launch HueForge.
2. Locate the image import area.
3. Select your PNG or JPG image.
4. Wait for HueForge to generate the initial preview.
5. Review how the image appears in the workspace.

Depending on your HueForge version, the import controls may look slightly different.

## Step 4: Adjust Image Dimensions

Once your image is imported, review its dimensions.

Consider:

- **Width and height:** Make sure your artwork fits your printer's build plate.
- **Aspect ratio:** Avoid stretching your image.
- **Level of detail:** Smaller prints may lose fine details.
- **Print orientation:** Consider how the final artwork will be displayed.

### Beginner Tip

Start with a relatively small test print before committing to a large, detailed project.

## Step 5: Review Your Image Preview

Before selecting filament colors, examine the initial preview.

Ask yourself:

- Is the main subject recognizable?
- Are important details visible?
- Are the highlights and shadows distinguishable?
- Does the background interfere with the subject?

If the preview doesn't look right, try editing the original image and importing it again.

## Step 6: Save Your Progress

Save your HueForge project so you can return to it later.

I recommend organizing your projects with descriptive names.

Example:

`portrait-blue-white-test`

This makes it easier to compare different versions as you experiment with filament colors and layer transitions.

## Common Problems and Solutions

| Problem | Possible Solution |
|---|---|
| Image looks blurry | Use a higher-resolution source |
| Details are difficult to see | Improve contrast |
| Background is distracting | Crop or simplify the background |
| Image looks stretched | Preserve the original aspect ratio |
| Preview looks too dark | Adjust source brightness and later review filament settings |
